fix(kassa): a negative goods-group count keeps its sign on the printed X and Z

The fit rule that stopped the printed X and Z cutting a count off a statutory figure
required an all-digit trailing group. A minus is not a digit, so the one count on this
document that can go below nought was the one count the rule excluded.

A goods group's quantity accumulates sign * line.Quantity, with sign -1 for a return,
while every statutory count on the paper accumulates += 1. A group whose returns
outweigh its sales nets negative, its label fails the all-digit test, and the whole
label including the count is shortened as prose:

    Sesongens smaksmeny (…       -1 234,00

Two columns wider the same row printed "Sesongens smaksmeny (-12…" for a quantity of
-123 - a return of twelve where there were a hundred and twenty-three. A count stated
nowhere invites a question; that one does not. And it is not an edge: a
returns-dominated day is an ordinary day.

TrailingCount now allows one optional minus after the opening parenthesis before
requiring digits, keeping the at-least-one-digit rule, so a signed count is excluded
from shortening exactly as an unsigned one is.

This is the third repair to this row at a scale that missed the next one. The first
shrank a label measured against a single world; the second protected unsigned counts
across five width regimes; and the sweep that proved the second - counts of 1, 9, 12,
127 and 1234 - never sampled a signed count. So the sign goes into the sweep and not
only into the rule: a signed goods group now rides all 25 cells, 30 count-bearing rows
per world instead of 29, and the reader that takes a count off the paper reads the
minus, so a truncated "(-12…" is reported as -12 against the count it replaced rather
than as no match at all. Five worlds printed by the reviewer are pinned by name, each
located by amount within its section and never by label, each asserting the count reads
back with its sign and its digits and that no ellipsis follows the last parenthesis.

Reverting the sign allowance reds 34 of the 913 Kassa tests and restoring it returns
all 913. Two neighbouring predicates in the same method - the digit test and the
empty-group guard - survive mutation because no label this builder composes can reach
them: the count is appended last and is always numeric.
